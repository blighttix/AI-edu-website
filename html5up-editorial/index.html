AI is BAD
Here is why...

By Arin Baum & Christopher Guevara

Since the release of publicly available AI in the form of generative chatbots such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Sora, and so much more, life has not been the same. AI, no matter how you put it has always had negative and positive backlash and commentary since AI became mainstream. While AI stands neutral on whether it is good or not, to talk about only the good aspects of it would not include all perspectives on it that may be needed. There are three major outliers that, once heard, may sway a person's opinion on the use and nature of Artificial Intelligence in question. These include the state of AI within the education system, the environmental impact, and finally what AI could do to harm the economy

Education

AI, while not inherently bad for the education system, still has some major risks when being used by both educators and students alike. For students, the excessive use of AI could lead to a case of codependency, leaving the student reliant on whatever chatbot they are using. Students who use AI to get the answers of their assignments rather than go through the problem-solving process themselves usually have a harder time learning necessary skills than others due to not exercising their thinking and problem-solving ability. For a developing child and teen, this is undoubtedly extremely harmful for their development, and they will probably end up really stupid.

Environment

The environmental impact that AI possesses is quite excessive as well. AI is run in data centers, temperature-regulated buildings used to store servers, data storage drives, and network equipment to run programs; these take up space on land that could have been used to build other essentials like housing. The amount of electricity that the machines powering AI need to operate causes a rapid increase in carbon emissions and adds pressure to electric grids. The amount of water alone to cool the hardware is extreme and can take water away from water sources and disrupt local ecosystems. In short, maintaining the needs of all the AI data centers needed to run AI.

Art

AI, while not seeming to have any large effect as of now, has already crept its way slowly but surely in the job market. Firstly, this can be seen most clearly with artists and web designers who are having their expertise taken by generative AI. Producers are losing their ground more and more, as the competition was already tight enough, but now with AI performing against other producers. This makes a situation where the only competitors left are above-average, high-quality producers who may charge over the average person's budget, or AI-produced media at a fairer price with lesser quality without the human touch.
